Binary Tree Traversals

Work through the four standard traversal orders on the given binary tree. Fill in each sequence, trace the level-order queue by hand, and answer the analysis questions. Show your reasoning — the queue trace is worth as much as the final sequences.

⏱ 60 minutes 🎯 50 points 👥 Individual work 🌳 17 nodes · depth 4

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab you should be able to:

  • Identify the root, children, leaves, and subtrees of a given binary tree.
  • Produce the preorder, inorder, and postorder sequences of a binary tree.
  • Perform a level-order (breadth-first) traversal using a queue.
  • Determine the depth of a tree and the nodes at each level.
  • Explain how traversal order depends on when the root is visited.

Part 2 — Traversal Rules (Reference) Given

Use these definitions to complete Parts 3–5.

Preorder Node → Left → Right Visit the node, then fully traverse its left subtree, then its right subtree.
Inorder Left → Node → Right Fully traverse the left subtree, visit the node, then traverse the right subtree.
Postorder Left → Right → Node Fully traverse both subtrees before visiting the node.
Level-order Queue (BFS) Visit the front of the queue, then enqueue its left child followed by its right child.

Part 6 — Analysis Questions 7 pts

Answer in complete sentences. Refer to specific nodes from Figure 1.

  1. Node E has a right child but no left child. Explain what this means about E's left subtree, and describe how E's position in the tree reflects this.

  2. Compare the preorder and postorder sequences. Where does the root node A appear in each, and why does it appear there?

  3. In the level-order traversal, when a node is dequeued, why must its left child be enqueued before its right child? What would change if the order were reversed?

  4. How many nodes are at the deepest level of this tree? What is the tree's depth, and which nodes are the deepest?
